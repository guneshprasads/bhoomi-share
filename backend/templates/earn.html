{{ 'ಹೂಡಿಕೆದಾರರಾಗಿ ಗಳಿಕೆ' if lang == 'kn' else 'Earning as an investor' }}

You put up the working capital for one crop, herd, batch cycle or share of a parcel. You earn by getting your costs back first and then taking the agreed percentage of what is left.

How the money reaches you

  • The plan lists its costs and its split before you register interest.
  • When the harvest, animals or batches sell, proceeds first repay the listed costs to whoever paid them.
  • Whatever is left is divided by the stated percentages — for example 70% to you, 30% to the grower.

What you need

  • Money you can afford to see tied up for a season, or lose.
  • An hour to read the plan properly: the parcel, the photographs, the budget, the grower’s log.

What can go wrong

  • Rain fails, pests arrive, animals fall ill, prices drop at the mandi.
  • A sale below the cost repays you only what there is. A failed season can return nothing.
  • Your money is tied up until the cycle ends.

{{ 'ಬೆಳೆಗಾರರಾಗಿ ಗಳಿಕೆ' if lang == 'kn' else 'Earning as a grower or keeper' }}

You have the skill and the land or space. Someone else funds the working capital. You earn the agreed share of what the season makes after the costs are repaid.

How the money reaches you

  • You post a costed plan: parcel, crop or animals, budget, expected sale, and the split you want.
  • You keep the agreed percentage of the surplus — what is left once the listed costs come out.
  • You log spend and progress so the people who funded you can see the season as it runs.

What you need

  • Real, honest costs. A plan that understates them is how seasons end badly.
  • Photographs and a named parcel, so a reader can picture it.

What can go wrong

  • If the sale does not cover the costs, your share is zero and your labour is unpaid.
  • You are accountable to the plan you posted. Surprises should go in the log early.

{{ 'ಭೂಮಾಲೀಕರಾಗಿ ಗಳಿಕೆ' if lang == 'kn' else 'Earning as a landowner' }}

Land sitting idle earns nothing. Licensed to someone who will farm it, on a fixed term and on paper, it earns rent and comes back to you on its date.

How the money reaches you

  • List the parcel: acreage, survey number, water source and hours, soil, road access, photographs.
  • Agree rent per acre per season, or a share of the crop, with a farmer who wrote to you.
  • Sign a fixed-term licence to cultivate, drafted against Karnataka tenancy law, and witnessed.

What you need

  • Your RTC (pahani) and survey details in order.
  • A clear view of who pays for borewell power, who mends the bunds and what happens if the rain fails.

What can go wrong

  • A rent is only as good as the farmer’s season. Agree what happens when it fails.
  • We do not collect rent or verify title. Have your own adviser read the licence.

{{ 'ರೈತರಾಗಿ ಗಳಿಕೆ' if lang == 'kn' else 'Earning as a farmer who needs land' }}

More land means more you can grow. The question to answer before you sign is simple: after inputs and rent, what is left per acre, and how bad a season can you survive?

How the money reaches you

  • Search parcels by district, size and what you can actually irrigate.
  • Write to the owner and agree rent or a crop share.
  • You keep what the crop sells for, minus inputs and rent.

What you need

  • Working capital for inputs. If you lack it, a can carry the season.
  • A realistic yield for that soil and that water.

What can go wrong

  • Rent is due even if the crop fails. The calculator shows your break-even: the revenue you need just to cover costs.
  • A licence ends on its date. Renewal is a decision, not something you can assume.

{{ 'ವೆಚ್ಚ ಮೊದಲು, ಉಳಿದದ್ದು ಹಂಚಿಕೆ.' if lang == 'kn' else 'Costs first, then the rest is split.' }}

Every funded plan on the site follows the same rule, so you can compare any two of them. Here it is worked through for a ₹{{ inr(sim.cost) }} plan expecting ₹{{ inr(sim.expected_sale) }}, funded 70/30.

  1. 1The harvest sells for ₹{{ inr(sim.expected_sale) }}.
  2. 2The listed costs of ₹{{ inr(sim.cost) }} come out first and go back to the funder.
  3. 3What is left, ₹{{ inr(sim.expected_sale - sim.cost) }}, is split 70/30: the funder takes ₹{{ inr((sim.expected_sale - sim.cost) * sim.investor_pct / 100) }}, the grower ₹{{ inr((sim.expected_sale - sim.cost) * sim.grower_pct / 100) }}.
  4. 4If the sale is below ₹{{ inr(sim.cost) }}, the funder takes back only what there is and the grower gets nothing for their labour.
