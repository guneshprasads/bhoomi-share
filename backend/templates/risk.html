Which plan runs short, and what does it cost?

Thousands of simulated seasons on each plan's own budget, yield and price. The chance the sale fails to cover the costs, the money that could be lost, and whether a fix would pay for itself.

A simulation, not a forecast. Every result comes from assumptions you can see and change below. Example data is invented.

1 · The risk

Will the sale cover the costs?

Each bar is the share of simulated seasons ending with that profit. Left of the line, the sale did not cover the costs. The funder is repaid first, so this is the funder’s shortfall.

2 · The fix

Which fix pays back?

A fix pays back when the value it creates is larger than what it costs. Every option is run on the same simulated seasons, so the difference is the fix and not luck.

3 · The whole holding

Where to act first

Every plan with the same assumptions. This is the view a funder or lender asks for: where the money is most at risk, and which fix earns the most.

4 · Can we trust the starting number?

One best estimate from three sources

The plan’s own figure, the land’s own history and a district benchmark are combined into one expected yield, each weighted by how precise it is. Better data narrows the risk above.

What is data, what is model, what is assumed

Built on the same example holding as the Farm ledger. Risk outputs are simulations from stated assumptions, not forecasts of any farm’s results, and not investment advice. No money moves through this site.
