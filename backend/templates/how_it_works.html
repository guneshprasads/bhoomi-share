Four separate things happen here and they do not depend on each other. Read whichever one brought you.

{% endif %}

1. Funding a crop season

One crop, one named parcel, one season. When it is sold, the proceeds are split the way the agreement says.

  1. 01

    A grower posts a plan

    Parcel, district and survey number, crop, sowing window, a costed input budget, the yield they expect, the split they want — and photographs of the field.

  2. 02

    You read it and register interest

    With an amount. Nothing is paid and nothing is binding — it tells the grower whether the plan is fundable.

  3. 03

    The season runs

    Sowing to harvest. The grower posts what was spent and what went in the ground, against the plan you read.

  4. 04

    The harvest sells and proceeds split

    Mandi or contract sale, receipts shared, split as signed. Costs come out first; what is left is divided.

2. Funding a livestock unit

Sheep, goat, dairy or poultry. The same shape as a crop plan, with a longer clock.

Somebody who keeps animals for a living runs the unit on their own land. You fund the animals, the feed, the shed and the vaccination; they do the work. A cycle is months rather than one harvest — nine months for a lamb crop, eighteen for a dairy unit to reach its first full lactation — and the plan says which.

  • Losses are part of the plan, not a footnote. Animals die. A plan that does not say what the keeper expects to lose is a plan you should not fund.
  • The animals belong to the unit, not to you personally, and the split is of what the cycle earns.
  • Vaccination and vet cover should be written into the budget. Ask if it isn’t.

3. Shares in a big parcel

Five acres or more, divided into equal shares so several people can take a piece.

The owner states what a year of work on the block costs — say twenty lakh for twenty-two acres — and what one share is worth, say twenty-five thousand. That makes eighty shares. Take one or take ten; your claim on the split is your shares divided by all the shares.

  • You are buying a share of one cycle’s work, not a piece of the land. No part of the survey number changes hands and nothing is registered in your name.
  • The number of people per parcel is capped, deliberately and visibly, and the page shows how many places are left. The reason is the fine print — this is the option closest to the line SEBI draws.
  • Shares are only offered on parcels of five acres and up. Below that, a crop plan does the same job with less complication.

4. Leasing land out

An owner who is not farming this year, a farmer who wants more land, and a fixed-term licence between them.

  1. 01

    List or search

    Acreage, survey number, water source and hours, soil, road access, what it will grow, photographs. Farmers search by district and by what they can irrigate.

  2. 02

    Agree on terms

    Rent per acre per season, or a share of the crop. Who pays for borewell power, who repairs the bunds, who keeps the residue, what happens if the rain fails.

  3. 03

    Sign a proper licence

    A fixed-term licence to cultivate, drafted against Karnataka tenancy law, signed and witnessed. Deliberately not an open-ended tenancy.

  4. 04

    Renew or move on

    At the end of the term the land comes back, with a record of how it was farmed. Renew if it went well; list it again if it did not.

What this site does and does not do

  • It puts the parcel or the plan in front of people and passes on your phone number when one of them writes to you.
  • It does not hold deposits, collect rent, move money, or execute any agreement. You sign on paper, with your own witnesses.
  • It does not verify title. Ask for the RTC (pahani) and read it, the way you would anyway.

What it costs

Nothing, during the pilot. We are not charging investors, growers, owners or farmers while we are still proving this on our own 15 acres. When there is a fee it will be stated on this page before it applies to anyone.

Where it runs

Karnataka, district by district — all 31 of them are in the lists, but we open them one at a time, after a full season of real numbers. The pilot is our family’s 15 acres in Belagavi district. Tell us your district and you will hear when it opens.
