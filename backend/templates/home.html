Land sitting idle is worth more with someone farming it.

Most of this already happens — by word of mouth, between families, on no paper at all. That works until it doesn’t. Four steps, and the third one is the whole point.

{% endif %}
01

List or search

Acreage, survey number, water source and hours, soil, road access, what was last grown, and photographs. Farmers search by district and by what they can actually irrigate.

02

Agree on terms

Rent per acre per season, or a share of the crop. Who pays for borewell power, who repairs the bunds, who keeps the residue, what happens if the rain fails.

03

Sign a proper licence

A fixed-term licence drafted against Karnataka tenancy law, signed and witnessed. Not a handshake, and deliberately not an open-ended tenancy — why that distinction matters.

04

Renew or move on

At the end of the term the land comes back to you, with a record of how it was farmed. Renew with the same farmer if it went well; list it again if it didn’t.

Two pieces of Indian law sit directly across this idea. Neither makes it impossible; both decide how it has to be built. We would rather put them on the front page than in a footnote — the long version is here.

Money

Small pools, and a lawyer’s name on the structure

Pooling money from the public and managing it for a share of the profits can qualify as a Collective Investment Scheme under the SEBI Act. So: capped pool size, a small number of people per plan, an LLP or partnership deed per cycle, and a counsel-reviewed structure before a single rupee moves. Shares in a big parcel are capped hardest of all, because that is the option closest to the line.

Land

State-aware terms, short by design

In Karnataka, as in several states, a tenant who farms the same land continuously can build up occupancy or purchase rights over it. That is exactly why landowners here avoid writing anything down. So the agreements are short fixed-term licences to cultivate, drafted against Karnataka law, with renewal as a decision rather than a default.

Proof

Proven on our own land first

Our 15 acres is the pilot. Our capital in the investor seat, our family in the grower seat, one parcel leased out on the licence we drafted. We want a full season of real numbers — including the parts that went badly — before we ask an outsider to fund a crop or hand over a field.
