Farm money is volatile, and nobody can see the bad case.

The data on why this problem is real, what we built for it, who we think would pay, and what we do not know yet. Written for people deciding whether to back it.

Read this first. This page describes a project. It is not an offer to invest, not financial advice and not a forecast. Figures in the charts are public data with sources below; anything about revenue is a hypothesis we have not yet tested, and we say so each time.

3 · The gap

The people who fund a season can’t see its bad case.

A budget that doesn’t add up, a season nobody logged, a yield claimed with no history, a price assumed flat. A funder is asked to trust numbers nobody has checked, so money is either not offered, or offered dear.

Idle

Land and skill without capital

Good land sits unfarmed and good farmers sit short of working capital. The two rarely meet on paper, because a handshake is the norm and writing anything down can build tenancy rights.

Opaque

Risk nobody can price

Without a checked account and a stated bad case, a lender cannot tell a careful plan from a careless one, so careful growers are priced like careless ones.

Volatile

Prices that swing

Section 1: a {{ w.swing }}-point range in food price inflation over the period. A flat-price budget is a hope, not a plan.

4 · What we built

One checked account per plan, and the bad case in plain view.

Check that every plan’s account adds up. Predict the chance a season falls short and the one-in-twenty bad case. Decide which fix pays back first.

  • Shown on an invented example farm, clearly labelled, with every assumption editable.
  • Prices today are an assumed swing. The step that makes this investable is replacing that assumption with real price history for Karnataka’s crops.
  • Bilingual (English and ಕನ್ನಡ), tested, and built to run on free infrastructure.

How we will find out

  • Finish the pilot season on our own 15 acres and publish the numbers, including the parts that went badly.
  • Check the model against reality: how often did a plan the tool called risky actually fall short?
  • Talk to lenders, producer organisations and insurers in Belagavi and write down what they would actually pay for.
  • Measure the time a reviewer saves on a plan whose account has already been checked.
6 · What backing would unlock

Milestones, in order.

  1. Counsel-cleared structure.Pools capped and small, licences fixed-term, nothing that resembles a public scheme.
  2. One district open.Belagavi first, with real plans, learning before opening a second.
  3. Real data in place of assumptions.Mandi prices (Agmarknet, e-NAM) and weather replace the assumed swing, so the risk numbers are measured.
  4. Reach.Kannada-first, low-bandwidth mobile, so growers use it without an intermediary.
  5. First paying pilots with a lender or producer organisation, if the interviews support it.

We deliberately give no amounts or timelines here: they should come from the pilot, not from this page.

7 · What could go wrong

Stated plainly.

Regulation

Pooling money can resemble a Collective Investment Scheme, and tenancy law shapes every land agreement. The product is built small and capped for that reason, and counsel review comes before money moves.

Trust and adoption

Growers must record honestly and funders must read the bad case. A tool nobody uses is worth nothing.

Model risk

A simulation can look precise and be wrong. Every number carries its assumptions, and the tool must be checked against real seasons before anyone relies on it.

Shared weather

Plans in one district can fail together in one bad monsoon. Diversifying across districts and models matters.
